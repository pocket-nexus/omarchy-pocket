Sheet 1 of 5Interactiondocs/interaction.md

Computers come in all shapes. So should Linux.

Not every computer has a keyboard, mouse, or desktop-sized screen.

Omarchy Pocket reimagines the Linux experience for devices with touchscreens, gamepads, physical buttons, and entirely different ways to interact.

Instead of shrinking a desktop, we design around the hardware.

The stage is the monitor.Takes no touch.
The deck is the keyboard.Changes with what it types into.
The gamepad is SUPER.A layer lasts as long as the shoulder is down.
A text field took the keyboardthe lock, a Wi-Fi password → keys
A card is on the stagethe menu, a panel, the picker → keys
A window has focusa touch app → pad; anything else → keys
Nothing has focusworkspaces and the panels → home

Keys

The keyboard: Esc, Tab, Ctrl, Alt and the arrows over four rows. The gamepad types the keys a thumb reaches without looking.

Pad

A trackpad for the pointer on the stage. A tap clicks, two fingers scroll, the Hold key drags.

Home

The workspaces on the left, the current workspace's windows in the middle, the bar's panels on the right.
