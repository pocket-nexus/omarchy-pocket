On the Equivalence of a $1,999 and a $94.99 Computer for the Purpose of Opening YouTube

The Omarchy Pocket ContributorsConflict of interest: the authors wrote the software on one of the two computers.

Abstract. We compare the iPhone Duo with an Anbernic RG DS Plus running Omarchy Pocket. The first exceeds the second by a factor of 26.8× in single-core performance and of 12 in memory, and costs 21× as much. Both open YouTube, X and GitHub, join a tailnet, route through a VPN, and offer a keyboard on their lower half. We conclude that the iPhone Duo is the better computer in 10 of 26 measures, that the two are equivalent in 7, and that the RG DS Plus has a D-pad.

1. Introduction

The folding pocket computer is an established form. Its recent examples place a keyboard on the lower half of the device and an application on the upper [1, 2]. Whether this arrangement requires a 2 nm process has not, to our knowledge, been studied.

2. Materials

Subject A is an iPhone Duo, released on 23 October 2026 at $1,999 [2, 4]. Subject B is an RG DS Plus, $94.99 at list [7], on which PocketJS and Omarchy Pocket replace the desktop graphics stack. Neither manufacturer took part.

3. Method

For Subject B, we opened each application on the device and observed that it opened. For Subject A, we did not obtain a unit, and rely on published figures [1–4] and on the App Store. This is a limitation. The sample size is two, one of each, and no statistical test applies.

4. Results

Table 1 gives the measured differences. Subject A exceeds Subject B in every measure of performance, by margins that leave little to discuss.

In the tasks tested (YouTube, X, GitHub, Tailscale, a VPN, palmtop use, voice input on the device) we observed no difference in outcome. On Subject B, windows moved at 60 fps with 557 of 953 MB free. Subject A refreshes at up to 120 Hz. In points of Geekbench per frame per second, Subject B required 2.9 and Subject A 39.4, a metric we devised for this paper and do not defend.

Points per dollar favour Subject A, at 2.36 to 1.85. We had hoped otherwise.

5. Discussion

For $1,999 one may purchase 21 units of Subject B, with 84 cores, 42 screens and a mass of 7.2 kg. Their multi-core scores sum to 10,395, which remains below that of one Subject A. Geekbench scores do not sum, and we report the figure regardless.

6. Threats to validity

The authors built the software on Subject B. They did not test Subject A. Subject B's Geekbench figures come from other RK3568 boards [5, 6]. YouTube was not asked for its opinion.

7. Conclusion

The iPhone Duo is the better computer. It has a camera, a modem and four times the battery life. Readers who do not need these may obtain Subject B.
